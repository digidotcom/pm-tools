# Example PPD: SOC 2 Type II for FleetCore Cloud (Fictional)

A **fictional** example PPD — Meridian Networks, FleetCore, and all competitors named here are invented; any resemblance to real companies is coincidental. Use this to gauge appropriate **tone, depth, and specificity** when drafting PPDs for **compliance, trust, or security positioning** — launches where the "product" is assurance, not a feature.

**What to study here:**
- Q1's primary customer is sharp: a specific buyer persona (IT admins, operations, compliance officers) in specific regulated verticals — not "enterprises."
- Q1 insights tie behavior directly to buying signals: certification as an RFP line item, not a nice-to-have.
- Q2's Why Now names external triggers (audit-cycle regulation, customer procurement shifts) — never "we've been planning this."
- Q2 splits internal credibility (win/loss, ticket data) from publishable proof (the audit report itself) — compliance launches are unusual in that the proof IS the product.
- Q3 handles the "competitors are less central" case honestly: the alternatives table compares *assurance postures*, and the named-competitor list is short and pruned.
- Q4 picks ONE benefit ("adopt without adding audit burden") and everything else supports it.
- Q5 metrics are measurable with clear methods (time-to-close deltas, RFP pass rates), not fluff.
- The Marketing Handoff turns an unglamorous compliance milestone into campaign-ready artifacts.

## Q1. (Listen) Who is the customer and what insights do we have about them?

### 1. Primary Customer
IT administrators, operations managers, and compliance officers at mid-to-large enterprises that manage distributed equipment fleets through FleetCore Cloud — concentrated in utilities, cold-chain logistics, and healthcare facilities, where connected infrastructure falls under formal security-review programs.

### 2. Insights on Them
These buyers answer to internal security and risk teams. Every new cloud platform they adopt triggers a vendor security assessment — questionnaires, evidence requests, sometimes on-site review. They increasingly treat independent certification as a shortcut through that process: a SOC 2 Type II report replaces weeks of custom evidence-gathering. The decision-makers are rarely the daily users; procurement and CISO organizations hold veto power.

### 3. Their Behavior
They buy on annual planning cycles with security review as a gating stage, not a formality — deals stall for 30–90 days in assessment. They favor vendors who make the review easy: a report they can hand to their auditor beats a promise. SOC 2 status increasingly appears verbatim as an RFP requirement; absence means disqualification before evaluation starts.

## Q2. (Define) What is the prevailing customer problem/opportunity? What data informed this?

### Customer Challenges
Customers face challenges with:
- **Vendor assessment burden:** every cloud platform adoption requires a custom security review, consuming security-team hours the customer would rather spend on their own posture.
- **Procurement stalls:** deals sit in security review for one to three months, delaying the operational improvements the platform was bought for.
- **Compliance inheritance:** customers must demonstrate to *their* auditors that upstream platforms meet controls — without vendor evidence, they absorb the gap themselves.
- **Rising bar:** connected-infrastructure attack surface keeps growing, and internal risk teams respond by tightening review requirements year over year.

### Problem Statement
Today, enterprise operations teams have to run manual security assessments and absorb compliance risk when adopting cloud platforms for fleet management. Customers need a way to adopt and integrate these platforms on the strength of rigorous, independently audited standards — cutting procurement friction and compliance overhead.

### Why Now — Market Timing
- **Market/regulatory driver:** enterprise customers' own audit regimes (SOC 2, ISO 27001, sector rules) increasingly require evidence for *upstream* SaaS dependencies — the requirement now flows down the supply chain.
- **Customer behavior change:** RFPs in our pipeline began listing SOC 2 as a hard requirement roughly two years ago; the share has grown every quarter since.
- **Competitive shift:** most fleet-management vendors in our segment still offer only self-attested security claims — a certification window exists before this becomes table stakes.

### Supporting Data — Internal Credibility
- Win/loss reviews attribute a meaningful share of stalled or lost enterprise deals to security-review friction (sales-cited, past 4 quarters).
- Support and sales-engineering time spent answering security questionnaires has grown steadily — each enterprise deal consumes SE days on evidence assembly.
- Three of the five largest open opportunities list SOC 2 as a procurement precondition.

### Supporting Data — Publishable Proof
- The SOC 2 Type II report itself — issued by an independent CPA firm, available to customers and prospects under NDA.
- Certification announcement and trust-center page (public).

### Customer Opportunity
If platform trust is independently verifiable, customers can compress vendor review from months to days, adopt operational improvements sooner, and satisfy their own auditors with inherited evidence instead of custom assessment work.

## Q3. (Invent) What is the solution? Why is it the right solution to address the customer need versus other alternatives?

### Solution (1-2 sentences)
FleetCore Cloud has achieved SOC 2 Type II certification — independent, audited validation that Meridian maintains strict controls for security, availability, and confidentiality across its cloud platform, sustained over the full audit period.

### Why This Solution vs. Alternative Approaches
- **Considered:** self-attested security whitepaper — rejected: doesn't survive customer audit teams; every claim still triggers custom verification.
- **Considered:** per-customer security questionnaires and evidence packages — rejected: unbounded SE cost, doesn't scale, repeats for every deal.
- **Considered:** ISO 27001 first — deferred: valuable, but North American enterprise procurement asks for SOC 2 first in our segment; ISO follows on the roadmap.

### Named Competitive Products
| Competitor | Their Positioning | How We Counter-Position |
|---|---|---|
| Ridgeline Telemetry | "Enterprise-grade security" (self-attested; no independent audit published) | Claims vs. evidence — our controls are independently audited over time, not asserted |
| Cobalt Fleet Systems | SOC 2 Type I (point-in-time) | Type II vs. Type I — we prove controls *operated effectively* across the audit period, not that they existed one day |

### Key Differentiators
The only platform in the segment with Type II (sustained-operation) certification covering security, availability, and confidentiality — combined with a public trust center and an NDA-ready report that drops directly into customer vendor-review workflows.

## Q4. (Refine) How would we describe the end-to-end customer experience? What is the most important customer benefit?

### Most Important Customer Benefit
Adopt FleetCore Cloud without adding audit burden — independent certification your security team can accept and your auditors can inherit.

### How This Solves the Customer's Pain
The problem in Q2 is assessment cost and stalled procurement. A Type II report substitutes for the custom review: security teams get auditor-grade evidence on day one, procurement gates clear in days instead of months, and the customer's own compliance program inherits upstream evidence instead of absorbing a gap.

### End-to-End Customer Experience
1. **Discover / Evaluate:** prospect finds SOC 2 status on the trust-center page; sales provides the report under NDA during evaluation.
2. **Acquire / Procure:** security review consumes the report instead of issuing a custom questionnaire; the RFP compliance line item is satisfied by evidence.
3. **Deploy / Install:** onboarding unchanged — certification rides along silently.
4. **Operate / Manage:** annual re-audit keeps the report current; customers reference it in their own audit cycles.
5. **Grow / Expand:** expansion purchases skip re-review — the vendor is already cleared.

### Supporting Customer Benefits
- **Faster procurement:** measurable reduction in security-review cycle time.
- **Lower internal cost:** security-team hours not spent on custom vendor assessment.
- **Audit inheritance:** upstream evidence flows into the customer's own compliance program.
- **Risk posture:** independent, recurring validation vs. one-time claims.

### Revenue Growth
- ARR from opportunities previously gated on certification (CRM tag at close)
- Win rate in RFPs listing SOC 2 as a requirement, before vs. after certification

### Strategic Positioning
- Trust-center page traffic and report-request conversion (web analytics)
- Inclusion in analyst/industry coverage of platform security in the segment

### Product Portfolio Performance
- Clean annual re-audit (no exceptions) — sustained certification is the metric
- Security-questionnaire volume per enterprise deal (should fall as the report substitutes)
