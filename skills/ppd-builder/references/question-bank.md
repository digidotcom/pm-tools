# Question Bank

Targeted questions for filling PPD gaps. Use during Step 5 (Draft First, Ask Second) — only ask questions for sections the PM's input didn't cover. Don't run through the full list; pick what's actually missing.

## How to Use This Reference

After drafting the PPD from available input, you'll have sections marked Covered, Partial, or Empty:

- **Covered** — skip entirely, no questions needed
- **Partial** — scan the questions below and ask only what fills the specific gap
- **Empty** — use the full question set for that section, but still batch 3-5 at a time

Most PPDs built from a completed PRD need fewer than 10 total interview questions. The highest-value questions to lean into are the **marketing-specific gaps**: named competitors, why-now timing, publishable proof, and Marketing Handoff artifacts. These are rarely in the PRD.

## Q1. (Listen) — Customer

### Primary Customer
- Who is the single primary customer segment? (industry, company type/size, geography, role)
- If there are multiple candidates, which one matters most for this launch?
- What's the buyer persona vs. user persona? (often different)
- Are there defining characteristics that sharpen the segment? (regulated industry, deployment environment, scale)

### Insights on Them
- What do these customers prioritize when making decisions? (rank-ordered if possible)
- Who are the key decision-makers? (technical managers, architects, procurement, C-level)
- What do they value? (standards compliance, vendor stability, TCO, ecosystem, support)
- What do they avoid? (vendor lock-in, proprietary tech, unproven solutions)
- What operational context defines them? (field conditions, scale, regulatory environment)

### Their Behavior
- How long is their planning and procurement cycle? (weeks, months, years)
- How do they buy? (direct, channel, OEM, distribution)
- What technology preferences do they have? (open standards, specific protocols, platform ecosystems)
- What tools and platforms do they already rely on? (existing NMS, cloud platforms, management systems)
- What does "field-proven" look like to them? (certifications, references, case studies, deployment count)

**Synthesis:** One primary customer paragraph + 4-6 bullet insights + 4-6 bullet behaviors.

## Q2. (Define) — Problem / Opportunity

### Customer Challenges
- What are the 3-5 specific challenges this customer faces today?
- Which challenge is the most painful? (cost, risk, complexity, time)
- What workarounds exist today?
- What's the cost of the status quo?

### Problem Statement
- Complete the sentence: "Today, [customers] have to [problem/opportunity] when [situation]. Customers need a way to [insert need]."
- Does this statement avoid naming the solution?

### Why Now — Market Timing (MARKETING CRITICAL)
This section is the campaign hook. Push for external triggers, not internal schedules.

- What has changed externally that makes this the right moment? Don't accept "we've been planning this for a while" — that's internal.
- Is there a **regulatory driver**? (new standard taking effect, compliance deadline, deprecation of old tech)
- Is there a **technology inflection**? (protocol reaching maturity, AI model capability threshold, hardware cost curve)
- Is there a **competitive shift**? (competitor exited category, pricing disruption, M&A event)
- Is there a **customer behavior change**? (AI adoption, remote work shift, procurement pattern change)
- If none of these apply, is this really a launch moment — or a roadmap update?

### Supporting Data — Internal Credibility
- What win/loss data supports this? (deal loss reasons, competitive displacement)
- What support ticket trends validate the problem?
- What does the sales team hear on customer calls?
- What internal market sizing exists?
- What product analytics show the pain? (usage patterns, drop-off points, workaround behavior)

### Supporting Data — Publishable Proof (MARKETING CRITICAL)
- Do we have **customer quotes** we can publish? Which customers have approved quotes or case study language?
- Are there **third-party analyst reports** we can cite? (Gartner, IDC, Forrester, industry analysts)
- Are there **public benchmarks or studies** we can reference?
- Are there **awards, certifications, or recognitions** to call out?
- Do we have **published case studies** from existing customers?
- If publishable proof is thin, is there time before launch to source some? (customer interviews with quote permission, analyst briefings)

### Customer Opportunity
- If the problem is solved, what becomes newly possible for the customer?

**Synthesis:** 3-5 challenges + problem sentence + Why Now bullets + internal credibility data + publishable proof data + opportunity framing.

## Q3. (Invent) — Solution

### Solution (High Level)
- In 1-2 sentences, what is the solution?
- What's the elevator pitch version? (not a feature list)
- Does the description survive being read aloud to a non-technical stakeholder?

### Why This Solution vs. Alternative Approaches
- What approaches were considered? (buy vs. build, partner vs. own, proprietary vs. standards-based, single-vendor vs. ecosystem)
- For each, why was it rejected?

### Named Competitive Products (MARKETING CRITICAL)
Push past generic categories. Marketing needs specific product names.

- Which **specific competitor products** do customers shortlist alongside ours? Name them.
- For each named competitor:
  - How do they position themselves? (their claimed strength, their headline benefit)
  - What does the prospect's evaluation rubric look like when we're both in the mix?
  - **How do we win against them in this segment specifically?** (not generically — in *this* customer context)
  - Where do they win against us? (honest competitive intel matters for battlecards)
- If the PM says "we don't really have direct competitors," push: what do customers use instead today? Those are the real competitors.

### Key Differentiators
- Why is Digi uniquely positioned to deliver this? (installed base, IP, ecosystem, standards participation, distribution, relationships)
- Be specific — "great ecosystem" is not a differentiator. "SOC 2 Type II–certified DRM platform with native integration across our entire cellular router portfolio" is.
- What's hard for a competitor to replicate quickly?

**Synthesis:** Solution sentence + approach alternatives + named competitor table (Product | Their Positioning | Our Counter) + differentiators.

## Q4. (Refine) — Experience & Benefit

### Most Important Customer Benefit (MARKETING CRITICAL)
This sentence likely becomes the campaign headline. Get it right.

- If customers could only remember one thing about this solution, what would it be?
- What's the benefit that most directly solves Q2's problem?
- Can you say it in one sentence? If not, it's not sharp enough.
- Does it lead with customer outcome, not product capability? ("Ship features 2x faster" beats "AI-powered code generation.")

### How It Solves the Pain
- Connect the benefit back to Q2's problem.
- What's the emotional payoff for the customer? (confidence, relief, speed, predictability)

### End-to-End Experience
- **Discover/Evaluate:** How does the customer first encounter this?
- **Acquire/Procure:** What's the buying motion?
- **Deploy/Install:** What's the first-day experience?
- **Operate/Manage:** What's day-to-day life with this solution?
- **Grow/Expand:** How does this evolve over time?

### Supporting Benefits
- What other benefits matter but aren't the headline? (3-5 typical)

**Synthesis:** 1 headline benefit + problem-solution linkage + 5-step experience + 3-5 supporting benefits.

## Q5. (Test & Iterate) — Success Metrics

Use Digi's four standard categories. Every metric needs a measurement method.

### Customer Adoption Metrics
- What unit counts indicate real adoption? (kits, accounts, activations, DRM signups)
- What new customer acquisition targets?
- What pipeline indicators? (large opportunities in the CRM, design wins, POCs)

### Revenue Growth
- What product revenue targets?
- What attach revenue? (DRM, service, accessory attach)
- What channel indicators? (distributor reorders, inventory turns)

### Strategic Positioning
- What ecosystem signals? (standards bodies, alliances, tradeshows)
- What media/analyst signals? (coverage, mentions, analyst reports, awards)
- What digital signals? (SEO rankings, page traffic, organic growth)
- What thought leadership? (whitepapers, webinars, case studies)

### Product Portfolio Performance
- What roadmap evolution signals? (new SKUs, features, extensions)
- What operational health metrics? (support tickets, firmware cadence, defect rates)
- What customer success signals? (renewals, expansion, NPS)

**Synthesis:** 3-5 metrics per category, each with a clear measurement method.

## Marketing Handoff (MARKETING CRITICAL — Build Last)

Synthesized from everything above. Not new data — re-presentation. Each artifact should be directly usable by marketing.

### Elevator Pitch (30-second verbal)
- Draft: "For [Q1 customer], who [Q2 problem], [product] is a [Q3 solution category] that [Q4 headline benefit]. Unlike [Q3 named competitors], we [Q3 differentiator]."
- Does it sound natural when spoken aloud?
- Does it survive a non-technical listener?

### Tagline Options
Generate 3-5 candidates. Let the PM react. Don't try to nail it on the first shot.

- What's the shortest version of the Q4 headline benefit?
- Is there a memorable metaphor or comparison that fits?
- Can we make it punchier? (taglines reward concision)
- Does it pass the "could a competitor also use this?" test? (if yes, it's not differentiated enough)

### One-Sentence Headline
- Max ~15 words. Landing page hero or press release lede.
- Leads with customer benefit, not product capability.
- Includes a concrete hook where possible. ("Connect AI Agents to your device fleet in minutes" beats "Powerful AI integration.")

### Target Search Keywords & Phrases
- What terms does the Q1 primary customer use when they search for this problem?
- What's the category name? (primary term)
- What problem-aware queries lead here? (long-tail)
- What competitor-adjacent terms matter? (competitor product names are legitimate SEM targets)

### Publishable Proof Points
- Pull directly from Q2 "Publishable Proof" section.
- Format each as a one-line statement with source attribution.

### Named Competitors (Battlecard Seed)
- Pull from Q3 "Named Competitive Products" table.
- Condense the counter-position to one line per competitor for sales quick-reference.

### Persona Messaging Angles
- For each key persona in the buying committee (typically 2-3), what's their primary concern?
- What messaging angle emphasizes the part of the positioning that resonates with that concern?
- Technical architect typically cares about performance, interoperability, control.
- Procurement/buyer typically cares about TCO, vendor stability, terms.
- Business owner/executive typically cares about outcomes, risk reduction, strategic fit.

## Bonus: Sanity-Check Questions

After drafting, ask these to stress-test the positioning:

- **Q1 ↔ Q2:** Does the problem in Q2 actually hit the customer in Q1 hardest? Or is it a generic industry problem?
- **Q2 ↔ Q3:** Does the Q3 solution directly address the Q2 problem?
- **Q3 ↔ Q4:** Does the Q4 most important benefit naturally flow from the Q3 solution?
- **Q4 ↔ Q5:** Do the Q5 adoption metrics actually measure whether customers are experiencing the Q4 benefit?
- **Q2 Why Now ↔ Handoff Headline:** Does the headline reflect the why-now urgency, or does it feel evergreen?
- **Q3 Named Competitors ↔ Handoff Battlecard:** Does each named competitor have a clean one-line counter?
- **Q2 Publishable Proof ↔ Handoff Proof Points:** Is there enough publishable proof to fill the Marketing Handoff section without inventing anything?
- **Whole narrative:** If you read Q1 → Q5 → Marketing Handoff aloud, does it sound like one coherent story marketing could run with tomorrow — or does it drift?

If any of these fail, loop back to the weakest section.
